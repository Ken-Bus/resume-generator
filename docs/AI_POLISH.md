# AI Polish ("Polish My Resume with AI"): as built

**Status:** implemented on the feature branch; **not merged or deployed**. Costs **one of the five included generations only when the polish meaningfully improves the wording**; a polish that finds nothing meaningful to improve costs nothing. No price or entitlement change.

**Where it appears:** the last step (Review) of the guided builder, **and at the top of the regular editor (Edit Fields)** for any licensed resume with something to polish, so it is easy to find after leaving the builder. Both places share one state: a polish started or finished in one shows in the other. Nothing is charged by navigating, typing, previewing, switching templates or editing.

**Customer flow**
1. The button states the cost: "Uses 1 of your N remaining generations."
2. A first click only opens a confirmation ("The AI will review your resume. A generation is charged only if it makes meaningful improvements to your wording. If it finds nothing meaningful to improve, or anything goes wrong, nothing is used"). Cancel does nothing.
3. A second, deliberate click ("Yes, polish my resume (uses 1)") sends the request. While it runs, the button and step navigation are locked.
4. The result replaces the wording in the editor. The page shows what was reworded (with **Restore my wording** per section and **Restore all my original wording**), and any line the AI added that the customer didn't provide is highlighted yellow in Edit Fields with "Added by AI: please make sure this is true".
5. The customer can then Tailor to a job (free, 5 runs) and download.

**Charging (`netlify/functions/polish-resume.js`, same model as Analyze)**
- All validation (code format, size, "is there anything to polish") runs **before** any reservation or AI call.
- `reserve_license_use` -> AI -> validate -> `commit_license_use`. Any failure -> `release_license_use`; provider outages never count toward the failure budget; the per-code throttle (6 costly failures per 15 minutes, 30 per day) applies unchanged.
- Issues the same signed `tailorToken`; Tailor remains token-gated, free, capped at 5 runs per paid generation.
- **Duplicates:** (a) the button is single-flight; (b) only one paid operation runs at a time per code (an Analyze or polish already in flight makes a second request return 409, uncharged); (c) a retry that reuses the `requestId` of a finished polish returns the same result without a second charge (best-effort: kept in memory on the serving instance for 5 minutes). A persistent idempotency key would need a database column; it was not added (no migration).
- No SQL migration. No new environment variable.

**The charge rule (owner-approved): only ACCEPTED, meaningful improvements are charged**
- The AI's answer is never applied as it arrives. `guardResume` (`_lib/integrity.js`) first produces the **accepted result**: facts come back from the customer's answers, invented entries are dropped, only known resume fields (as text) survive, and **every line the integrity checks flag is put back to the customer's own wording ("withheld")**. The result is re-checked until nothing is flagged, so what is accepted is clean by construction.
- **Meaningful = at least one accepted line was REWRITTEN** (`assessChange` / `classifyEdit`, `netlify/functions/_lib/integrity.js`). The test is the SHAPE of the edit, never the length of the line or a word count:
  - **cosmetic**: only capitals, punctuation or spacing differ, or the same lines were re-ordered: not charged.
  - **minor**: ONE word added, removed or swapped in ONE place (for example "Helped organize food for pickup" -> "Helped organize **donated** food for pickup", or "Helped" -> "Assisted"), or only filler words ("and", "more", "with"...) changed: not charged. Any number of such touch-ups together is still not charged.
  - **rewrite**: anything else (two or more separate edits, a reordered or restructured sentence, several words replaced, a new line): charged.
  - Lines are matched by content inside their group, so deleting a duplicate or re-ordering a list never makes the other lines look rewritten; **deleting text alone is never charged**.
  - Why this rule and its limits: see `docs/MEANINGFUL_IMPROVEMENT_POLICY.md` (single proposal, alternatives, tradeoffs).
- **Nothing accepted** (the AI changed nothing, or only capitals/punctuation, or only touch-ups, or only deleted text, or every change was withheld): the reservation is **released uncounted** (`release_license_use(id, false)`), so the customer is charged zero and it never touches the database failure budget (6 per 15 minutes, 30 per day). The server answers `{ noChange: true, charged: false, reason: 'unchanged' | 'flagged', withheld: [...] }` with the unchanged counter and **sends no text back**. No Tailor token is issued (nothing was spent); a token from an earlier paid generation stays valid.
- **Something accepted:** commit exactly one generation and return the accepted result, the Tailor token, and `withheld` (where and why, **without the AI's text**).
- **Atomicity:** one reservation per attempt; the guard and the decision run before the commit, so the outcome is exactly one of *committed* or *released*. A commit that cannot be recorded is released uncounted (ref P106) and the result is not returned. A fault in our own guard fails closed: released uncounted, nothing charged, no AI text shown (ref P107). A retry with the same `requestId` returns the same answer without a second AI call or charge.
- **No free AI:** every uncharged outcome (unchanged, capitals-only, deletion-only, all-withheld) is capped per code (`_lib/nochange.js`: 3 per 15 minutes, 10 per day; checked before any reservation or AI call). Withheld suggestions are described, never handed out, so a rejected suggestion cannot be harvested for free. **Limit:** the cap is in memory per serverless instance (hash of the code, never the code), so it is best effort across instances; the database already allows only one paid operation at a time per code. A durable, cross-instance cap needs a new column on `license_reservations` (production schema change, not approved).
- **Diagnostics (counts only, never text or the code):** ref P201 (nothing to improve) and P202 (everything withheld) in the function log, plus one `ai-outcome` line per attempt (`charged` / `nochange` / `flagged` / `failed`).

**One attempt, one outcome (page)**
- Every polish attempt ends in exactly one of: improved (green, with Restore controls), no meaningful improvement (yellow, no generation used) or an error (red). All three go through `Wiz.setOutcome`, and a new attempt clears the last one's result. If an earlier polish is still in the resume and the latest attempt was not an improvement, a "Restore all my original wording" link stays available.
- Bug fixed here: a no-change reply used to fall through to the failure handler and show the generic red "Something went wrong while polishing" next to the yellow notice (found in a real-browser test on f7ef615). No-change is a success path and never reaches `polishFailed`.

**Why a clean resume can come back unchanged (Sonnet 5.5 run, Oct 22 2026: reworded 0 / cosmetic 0 / unchanged 9)**
- Guardrails are not the cause: honest rewordings of plain lines are kept exactly as written, counted as meaningful and not flagged; embellishments are still flagged (`writing-quality.test.js`).
- The input was already clean (rough text would have produced at least capitalization changes), so returning it unchanged is partly correct behavior.
- The prompt also gave a clean line no reason to change: one escape clause and examples that only showed rough fragments. Rule 5 now names what can still be improved using the person's own details (grammar and punctuation, vague verbs, wordy or repeated lines, details from the same entry), says never to reword a line just to change it, and adds two examples (one improved from the entry's own details, one correctly left alone). FACT RULES are byte-identical (hash pinned in a test).
- Effect on real model output is **not yet verified** (no paid call was made). The no-change protection still applies if the model changes nothing.

**What the integrity checks flag (AI Polish and Tailor)**
- Found in the Sonnet 5.5 acceptance run (fictional Taylor Rivera, commit 9121379): a blank description filled in, a beneficiary and outcome borrowed into the wrong entry, and "helped organize" turned into "organized" all passed, because every line was compared with the vocabulary of the WHOLE resume (so "Community Food Pantry" in one entry made "families" and "community" look supported in another), and role inflation was never checked.
- Each line is now compared with its OWN entry. Flagged: text added where the customer wrote nothing; a beneficiary ("for families", "in the community", "to support ...") not named in that entry; an implied result or impact ("resulting in", "ensuring", "improving" ...) not stated there; a supporting role ("helped", "assisted", "was part of") turned into ownership ("organized", "planned", "ran", "built", "created"); an ownership verb that appears nowhere in the entry; **a line copied verbatim from a different entry**; two or more content words the entry never used when one of them is the customer's word from another entry; plus the older checks (numbers, leadership/credential/praise wording, new skills, two or more words that appear nowhere).
- Tailor uses the same guard (`mode: 'tailor'`), on the cleaned resume. If the AI re-ordered bullets (Tailor does, on purpose) a flagged bullet puts the whole group back rather than duplicating one line and losing another. Tailor's "matched keywords" list is checked against the accepted resume; anything not actually in it moves to "not covered". Tailor never uses a generation, so withholding only changes what is applied.
- Analyze is checked against the pasted text (`groundAnalyzed`): an email, phone, website or year that is not in the text is removed (ref A201); a number in a bullet that is not in the text is flagged yellow with the reason.
- Prompt FACT RULES 5-7 say the same (keep the level of involvement; no beneficiaries or results not stated in THIS entry; blank stays blank). Rules 1-4 are unchanged (hash pinned). Edit Fields marks flagged project descriptions and job summaries as well as bullets.
- Known limits: pattern based. A fabricated claim in plain words that avoids every pattern can still pass, and an honest line that uses a pattern word not in its entry will be withheld (the customer can type it in themselves). The wording of unfaithful suggestions is never shown, only where and why.

**Writing quality (`polish-resume.js` prompt)**
- **Audience.** The customer says which fits them on step 1 (first resume / I've worked before / changing to different work); otherwise it follows from the answers (no jobs = first resume). The prompt sends only the matching guidance: *first resume* draws on school, volunteering, projects and training without making them sound like paid work; *experienced* makes duties specific and scannable, keeps long tenure and dates, and never turns routine duties into achievements; *career changer* words real duties so what carries over is clear, and never claims experience in the new field. Unknown values are ignored, and a choice that makes no sense (e.g. "experienced" with no jobs) falls back to the answers.
- **Style.** Natural, modest, professional language; real improvement (say what was done, with what, using the customer's own words and meaning) rather than added adjectives; a named list of generic AI phrases to avoid unless the customer wrote them; concrete good/bad examples in the prompt. The goal text is length-limited and stripped of quotes so it cannot alter the instructions.

**Truthful content (`_lib/integrity.js`)**
- The AI only receives the customer's own answers, with a prompt that forbids new facts, numbers, credentials, leadership or entries.
- Facts (name, contact, employers, titles, dates, schools, degrees, certifications, awards, locations) are **restored deterministically** from the answers after the AI returns; invented jobs/projects/volunteer entries are dropped; a dropped entry is put back.
- Lines with numbers, leadership wording or credential wording not in the answers, new skills, and **any AI-written headline or summary** are flagged for the customer to confirm. Flags are heuristic: they warn, they never delete. An empty work history stays empty and is never an error.

**Known limits**
- The flags are keyword/number based. A made-up claim in plain words that avoids every listed word and uses fewer than two unfamiliar words can still pass, and an honest rewording that uses two unfamiliar words will be flagged; the customer always has Restore.
- If the reply is lost after the charge, the customer is told to check the counter; retrying with the same request id on the same warm instance will not charge again, but a different instance cannot know about the first.
